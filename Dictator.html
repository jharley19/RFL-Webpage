Biography

The Dictator is recognized as the founding franchise of the RFL. Since the league’s inception, team owner Jeremy Harley has also served as the league’s long-standing commissioner, overseeing operations while building one of the most successful dynasties in RFL history.

The Dictator began their reign of dominance in 2013, claiming the inaugural RFL Championship. They led the league in total points and set a single-week scoring record of 156.48 points, a mark that stood unchallenged for five years until the introduction of half-PPR scoring.

In 2014, The Dictator set out to defend their title, once again finishing the season with the most points and an impressive 12–2 record. However, their bid for a repeat fell short in the championship game against The Huntingtown Hurricanes. Determined to reclaim the throne, The Dictator entered 2015 with revenge on their mind.

The 2015 season saw the rivalry between The Dictator and The Huntingtown Hurricanes reach its peak. Although The Hurricanes led the league in points that year, The Dictator finished first in the regular season at 11–3 and advanced to their third straight championship appearance. In a dramatic rematch against their rival, The Dictator prevailed, earning their second league championship in three years.

The 2016 season marked another dominant campaign, with The Dictator once again finishing first in the regular season and leading the league in scoring for the third time in four years. However, their playoff run fell short, as they suffered a disappointing first-round exit, the first time in franchise history they failed to reach the championship game.

In 2017, The Dictator sought redemption. Despite a modest 8–6 record, they once again led the league in total points, the fourth time in five seasons, and fought their way back to the championship match, their fourth appearance in five years. Facing off against the Cookie Charizards, the winners of the previous year’s championship, The Dictator trailed by 11.40 points heading into Monday Night Football with only one player remaining: DeAndre Hopkins. With third-string quarterback T.J. Yates under center and the Texans down 27–0 late in the fourth quarter, hope seemed lost. But on first-and-goal, Yates tossed a fade to Hopkins, who made an unbelievable one-handed bobbling touchdown catch in the back of the end zone, securing The Dictator’s third championship in five years in storybook fashion. The Dictator attributed this improbable championship run to their good-luck charm, Scabby Katie, a girl they befriended despite her odd habit of eating her own scabs. By showing her kindness and offering high fives, The Dictator claimed their team’s performance consistently improved, cementing the legend of one of the most bizarre superstitions in RFL history.

In 2018, Scabby Katie evolved from a mere lucky charm into a full-blown goddess in the eyes of The Dictator. This newfound devotion fueled another dominant season, as The Dictator once again led the league in scoring — despite what was widely regarded as the worst draft in the league that year. However, their playoff run was cut short in the first round due to a string of untimely injuries to key players, resulting in a 3rd-place finish. Outraged by the collapse, The Dictator turned on Scabby Katie, blaming her for the downfall and even going so far as to burn her picture in a symbolic act of betrayal. During the offseason, while taking a contemplative stroll with trusted companion Lord Brian, The Dictator came upon a mysterious message — one he interpreted as a divine revelation proclaiming The Lord of Light as the true fantasy god.

The 2019 season was thus declared a holy crusade, wholly devoted to The Lord of Light. To honor his new deity, The Dictator “spit straight fire” by releasing a diss track for every other team in the league, including the infamous and award-winning “Mike Diss”, which was ultimately removed from YouTube for reasons still debated to this day. Despite his unwavering devotion and lyrical vengeance, The Dictator’s faith went unrewarded. The Lord of Light abandoned him, and The Dictator missed the playoffs for the first time in franchise history, finishing a disappointing 6th overall.

The lead up to the 2020 season was one of great self-reflection. The Dictator eventually reached an epiphany: there is no fantasy god — there is only The Dictator. Reborn in his own image, he entered the 2020 season seeking to reclaim his former glory. To help manage operations, he hired an eager young intern, whom he dubbed “The Dictern.” That season, The Dictator launched a weekly propaganda series titled “7 Minutes of Hate”, where he dissected league events, aired grievances, and preached the greatness of his franchise. Despite his fiery rhetoric, the season itself was one of the darkest in The Dictator’s reign. Struggling from start to finish, he posted the worst record in team history, narrowly avoiding humiliation by defeating The Red Army in the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. The poor performance prompted swift action: The Dictator fired The Dictern, declaring that only he alone was worthy of bearing the weight of The Dictator’s empire.

The 2021 season began with a shocking revelation. The Dictern, The Dictator’s former intern, had deliberately sabotaged the 2020 season, and now plotted to do the same in 2021. Meanwhile, The Dictator had entered therapy, seeking to understand the chaos that had consumed his once-great empire. During a session, while examining a series of inkblots, he experienced a spiritual breakthrough. The visions compelled him to undertake a pilgrimage to the holy land, the very site where three years earlier he had burned the picture, and thus the soul—of Scabby Katie. Upon arriving, The Dictator fell to his knees and delivered a tearful, emotional apology, begging for Scabby Katie’s forgiveness. As he wept, a baseball bat materialized before him, humming with a strange energy. Approaching cautiously, The Dictator realized that the bat was now inhabited by the spirit of Scabby Katie. Just as he reached for it, betrayal struck. From the shadows emerged The Dictern, who slashed The Dictator’s wrist and revealed his twisted plan for vengeance. Seething with resentment over his past mistreatment, The Dictern declared his intent to kill The Dictator and claim his title. Before pulling the trigger, he chose instead to torture him, driving the tip of his rifle into The Dictator’s left eye. As The Dictator screamed in agony, the bat began to quiver—then violently shook free, flying through the air and striking The Dictern, knocking him to the ground. The bat landed firmly in the grasp of the fallen Dictator, who rose to his feet, reborn in fury. Terrified, The Dictern fled into the forest as The Dictator collapsed from his wounds. Fortunately, The Dictffcer, ever-loyal and suspicious of The Dictator’s lone journey, had followed him. He recovered the unconscious leader and rushed him back to headquarters, where The Dictator underwent recovery and transformation. The once-iconic shirt and tie were replaced with a bulletproof vest and an eyepatch, a new look symbolizing a time of war. Before turning his attention to the league, The Dictator first sought to eliminate the internal threat. A search party led by The Dictffcer tracked The Dictern as he plotted a bombing of The Dictator’s headquarters. The traitor was captured and brought before The Dictator himself to answer for his crimes. Despite The Dictern’s pleas for mercy, The Dictator sentenced him to death, a sentence he carried out personally with Scabby Katie in hand. With that dark chapter finally closed, The Dictator, once again united with his divine weapon, focused on reclaiming what was rightfully his. The 2021 season began with renewed hope and one of The Dictator’s strongest drafts in years. Yet fate intervened cruelly, as J.K. Dobbins and Gus Edwards both tore their ACLs before the season even began. Early struggles followed, but through a series of shrewd midseason trades, The Dictator rebuilt his roster with Keenan Allen, Jalen Hurts, and Nick Chubb. This new core ignited a late-season surge, as The Dictator won three straight games to close the regular season and snuck into the playoffs for the first time since 2018. In the semifinals, a clutch Monday Night Football performance by Alvin Kamara pushed The Dictator past The Maryland Marijuanas and back into the championship game for the first time since 2017. Though The Dictator ultimately fell short of reclaiming his throne, the season marked a triumphant rebirth, a return to power forged in blood, betrayal, and redemption.

The 2022 offseason opened with a rare glimpse inside The Dictator’s war room. During a strategic presentation led by The Dictffcer, The Dictator appeared distracted, haunted, even. Without warning, he stood up mid-briefing and walked upstairs to fetch a drink. While there, he decided to heat a blueberry scone in the microwave. As the microwave buzzed, the sound triggered a flood of PTSD flashbacks from the brutal 2021 championship battle. In the flashback, The Dictator had burned the marijuana fields of The Maryland Marijuanas, crippling their supply lines. But the act came at a cost, most of The Dictator’s own forces were left dazed and unfocused from exposure to the smoke. Undermanned but undeterred, The Dictator pressed on to face The Hurts Helicopters in a final confrontation. As he took aim at the incoming aircraft, Scabby Katie suddenly pushed him out of the path of a missile strike. The blast knocked The Dictator unconscious, and with their commander down, The Hurts Helicopters claimed victory and the championship. Snapping back to reality, The Dictator seethed with resentment, placing blame for the loss squarely on Scabby Katie. He hadn’t spoken to her since. Concerned, The Dictffcer gently suggested couples therapy to repair their fractured bond, but The Dictator dismissed the idea and chose a far more radical solution: Dictation Island. The Dictator and Scabby Katie joined the cast of a reality TV show designed to test their relationship. For one month, The Dictator lived in a villa surrounded by single women, while Scabby Katie lived in a house full of single men. Throughout the show, The Dictator flirted with temptation but soon realized that none could compare to Scabby Katie. Meanwhile, Scabby Katie embarked on her own journey of self-discovery and emotional healing, forming a close connection with a single man named Salvator. As the experiment came to an end, the two were reunited for a final bonfire ceremony. There, The Dictator made a tearful, heartfelt plea, admitting his mistakes and professing his undying love. Moved by his sincerity, Scabby Katie forgave him. The two reconciled, their bond restored as they entered the 2022 RFL season united once more. The season began rocky, with The Dictator starting 2–2, but the renewed partnership soon ignited a six-game winning streak, propelling them to 8–2 and the brink of the top seed. In the final week of the regular season, The Dictator faced their longtime rival Big Ounce in the first-ever Rivalry Week Mario Bowl. In a dramatic, back-and-forth battle, The Dictator pulled off a comeback victory behind a massive performance from QB Kirk Cousins and WR Justin Jefferson, securing the #1 seed. In the semifinals, The Dictator faced The Mullet Man and again triumphed, powered by another electric showing from the Cousins–Jefferson duo. This victory set up a championship clash with Sleepy Nick. Unfortunately, fate once again intervened. The Packers’ defense completely neutralized Cousins and Jefferson, and despite the unprecedented Damar Hamlin incident limiting Sleepy Nick’s QB Joe Burrow and WR Tee Higgins to zero points, The Dictator still came up short. It was a heartbreaking finish, second place for the second straight year, but The Dictator’s empire had fully risen again. With Scabby Katie restored by his side and his forces reunited, the world knew one thing for certain: The Dictator would be back.

Entering the 2023 season, The Dictator had vanished. No press conferences. No social media posts. No public appearances. Even his longtime companion, Scabby Katie, was nowhere to be found. Whispers spread through the league, had The Dictator fallen? Or was he simply plotting in silence? Whatever the truth, the season began as a nightmare. The Dictator stumbled out of the gate, losing six of his first seven games. The empire was crumbling. But just when it seemed all hope was lost, The Dictator had a resurgence armed with two critical acquisitions: free-agent QB Dak Prescott and breakout RB Rachaad White. By Week 12, The Dictator had clawed back to 6–6, fighting his way back into playoff contention. But the momentum soon faltered. Back-to-back lackluster performances dropped him to 6–8, officially ending his playoff hopes by Week 15. Even in elimination, The Dictator found pride in tradition, defeating rival Big Ounce in the Mario Bowl, led by dominant performances from Rachaad White and WR Rashee Rice. The Dictator entered the Toes Bowl as the 7th seed, facing The Wack Attacks. But despite his best efforts, WR Amari Cooper’s monstrous 51.10 point outburst sealed The Dictator’s fate. In Week 17, he narrowly lost to Sleepy Nick, closing the book on a turbulent campaign with an 8th-place finish.

Determined to rebound, The Dictator entered 2024 in near-total silence once again. No statements. No appearances. Only whispers that he was training in the shadows, rebuilding both his body and his empire. Despite the quiet, the team performed admirably. By Week 15, The Dictator stood at 8–6, well within reach of a playoff berth. But fate demanded another test, the third annual Mario Bowl, a must-win clash against his greatest rival, Big Ounce. The Dictator came armed for war. Amon-Ra St. Brown and Mike Evans each erupted for nearly 40 points, delivering the kind of offensive firepower worthy of a legend. Yet it wasn’t enough. Big Ounce’s QB Jared Goff delivered a heroic performance, handing The Dictator his first-ever Mario Bowl loss and eliminating him from playoff contention. Despite the heartbreak, The Dictator continued to fight. In the Toes Bowl, he defeated The Hurts Helicopters in the opening round but suffered an excruciating 0.16 point loss to The Maryland Marijuanas in the finals, finishing the season in 6th place. It was a humbling two-year stretch for the once-great ruler. The Dictator’s silence had become his armor, but in the shadows, a familiar flame still burned. Scabby Katie had yet to return, but those close to the empire knew one thing: The Dictator was preparing for something big.

By 2025, it had been three long years since anyone had seen or heard from The Dictator. No speeches, no sightings, no Scabby Katie. His once mighty empire had fallen silent, its banners faded, its throne collecting dust. Yet when the season began, whispers spread through the league: The Dictator still lives. But if he did, his team showed few signs of life. By the end of Week 3, The Dictator sat at 1–2, looking destined to miss the playoffs for a third consecutive season. Desperate to turn things around, The Dictator made a bold midseason trade, sending star TE Trey McBride and WR Jameson Williams to The Hurts Helicopter in exchange for RB Bucky Irving and WR Quentin Johnston. The move was a gamble, an all-in attempt to restore the power of the once-feared regime. At first, it seemed like a masterstroke. But the football gods were not kind. After Week 4, Bucky Irving suffered a long-term injury that would sideline him until Week 13. To make matters worse, second-overall pick Ceedee Lamb went down with a severe ankle injury, forcing him to miss four crucial weeks. The injuries crippled The Dictator’s campaign. By Week 12, the team had fallen to 5–7, their playoff hopes hanging by a thread. One more win could have saved them from the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, but fate had other plans. Back-to-back losses dropped The Dictator to 5–9, sending them tumbling toward another dark chapter. In Rivalry Week, The Dictator faced his eternal nemesis, Big Ounce, in the fourth Mario Bowl. Big Ounce, sitting at 4–10, was also fighting to escape the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. Despite The Dictator’s confidence, Bucky Irving and TE Isaiah Likely both delivered disastrous performances, allowing Big Ounce to claim a second consecutive Mario Bowl victory, and condemning The Dictator to the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl once again. There, The Dictator faced Gruden’s Plan, a team that had stumbled to a miserable 3–12 record. Though fallen from grace, The Dictator still had enough firepower to avoid total humiliation, defeating Gruden’s Plan and closing the season in 9th place. But the victory was hollow. The Dictator had now missed the playoffs three straight years, the longest drought in franchise history. Once a symbol of dominance and fear, The Dictator’s empire stood fractured, its leader still shrouded in silence. As the curtain fell on 2025, only one question lingered across the RFL: Had The Dictator’s reign truly ended, or was this the calm before the storm?
