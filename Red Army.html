Biography

The Red Army entered the league in its inaugural season in 2013 and remains one of only two teams to have participated in every season since the league’s inception. In their first year, The Red Army struggled to find consistency, finishing the regular season 5–8 in fifth place. They earned a spot in the play-in game against the Huntingtown Hurricanes, where they cruised to victory and clinched their first playoff appearance. Matched up against Big Ounce in the postseason, The Red Army suffered a narrow defeat that sent them to the consolation game, ultimately finishing their inaugural season in fourth place.

The 2014 season brought hopes of redemption, but the results were familiar. The Red Army finished with a 6–8 record, once again landing in fifth place. With the league expanding to eight teams, this was not enough to qualify for the playoffs, and they narrowly missed the postseason for the first time in franchise history.

In 2015, The Red Army posted what was then their best regular season, finishing 8–6. However, for the second straight year, that record still placed them fifth, leaving them on the outside looking in. Despite missing the playoffs, they dominated the consolation bracket, winning back-to-back Toes Bowl Championships and closing the season at 10–6, reinforcing their growing reputation as the kings of the lower bracket.

Entering 2016, The Red Army aimed to escape the cycle of mediocrity and return to the playoffs for the first time in three years. That hope faded quickly, as they managed just two wins through the first eight weeks. A late-season resurgence helped them claw back to a 6–8 finish, once again landing in fifth place. Still, their dominance in the Toes Bowl continued, as they captured their third straight Toes Bowl championship, further cementing their strange legacy of postseason success without playoff qualification.

By 2017, frustration had fully set in. After five consecutive seasons of narrowly missing the playoffs, The Red Army entered the year with a chip on their shoulder. That motivation paid off, as they posted a 9–5 record and finally returned to the playoffs, finishing second place in the regular season. It marked their first postseason appearance since 2013. However, the return was short-lived. Facing the Cookie Charizards, The Red Army failed to show up and were blown out by more than 50 points, ending their run abruptly. They closed the season 9–7 and finished in fourth place, satisfied to be back but disappointed with the result.

The momentum failed to carry into 2018. Attempting to replicate their previous success, The Red Army instead stumbled out of the gate, winning just one game in their first six weeks. The season unraveled from there, finishing 4–10 in eighth place, their worst result to date. That placement sent them back into the Toes Bowl bracket, where they had historically thrived. With a 6–0 all-time record in Toes Bowl matchups and three championships, expectations were high once again. They reached the Toes Bowl championship game but were finally defeated by The Hurts Helicopter, snapping their reign. The Red Army ended the year 5–11 and in sixth place, closing a deeply disappointing season.

The 2019 season marked one of the strangest chapters in franchise history. The Red Army underwent a dramatic name change and released an iconic video criticizing the league and its commissioner for the removal of Tyler Ulmschneider and the Cookie Charizards. In the video, the owner declared Tyler the true fantasy god and even renamed his own team the "Cookie Charizards" in tribute. He also delivered the now-infamous line, “Gruden has no plan,” a direct shot at Gruden’s Plan. Despite this newfound devotion, the season quickly spiraled. The team managed just one win through seven weeks and finished 5–9 in ninth place. That finish placed The Red Army in the first-ever Panzerkampfwagen Bowl against The Hurts Helicopter. After a grueling two-week battle, The Red Army emerged victorious, narrowly avoiding the league’s inaugural punishment. In the aftermath of another brutal season, the franchise abandoned Tyler as a false idol, closing the chapter on one of the most chaotic and memorable eras in RFL history.

Leading up to the 2020 season, The Red Army formally rejected Tyler, but their skepticism toward the league and its commissioner only intensified. This culminated in another video, released as a manifesto, in which The Red Army called upon league members to unite and reject Commissioner Jeremy Harley. In the video, Brian portrayed the commissioner as a fascist leader acting solely in his own self-interest and indifferent to the will of the common league member. He instead advocated for a communist governing structure in which no individual held power over another, acknowledging Karl Marx as his new fantasy god and officially adopting the team name The Red Army. On the field, the season began promisingly, as The Red Army won three of their first four games. This early success was fueled by an excellent draft, which earned them the league’s Best Draft Award, and a series of fortunate injuries. Marlon Mack’s Achilles tear in Week 1 allowed Jonathan Taylor to take over the Colts backfield, while Nick Chubb’s MCL sprain increased Kareem Hunt’s workload. However, the good fortune quickly faded. The Red Army managed just two wins over their remaining ten games, finishing the regular season 5–9 in ninth place. This placed them in back-to-back Panzerkampfwagen Bowls, this time against rival The Dictator. The Red Army was defeated and finished in last place, triggering a brutal punishment in which every league member was allowed to throw a pie in the face of owner Brian Levesque. No public word has been heard from him since that humiliating ordeal.

The Red Army entered the 2021 season desperate to escape the league’s basement and restore credibility. Unfortunately, the strong draft from the previous year was replaced by what was widely considered the worst draft in the league. Questionable decisions plagued the draft board, including selecting George Kittle in the second round over Travis Kelce and reaching for Laviska Shenault Jr. in the eighth round. These missteps left the team in a hole from the start, and The Red Army managed only three wins through their first ten games. They finished the regular season 6–9 in ninth place, once again relegated to the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, their third straight appearance. Facing Goofy God, The Red Army survived a hard-fought matchup and narrowly avoided last place, closing the season in ninth.

By 2022, The Red Army had become something of a league laughingstock, having finished last or second-to-last in three consecutive seasons. However, the year appeared to signal a potential turning point. By rivalry week in Week 15, The Red Army sat at 8–6 in fourth place, firmly in the playoff hunt. In a crucial matchup against rival Sleepy Nick in the inaugural Manchester Bowl, The Red Army held a comfortable lead heading into Monday Night Football. That lead evaporated when Aaron Jones erupted for 20.60 points, powering a comeback victory for Sleepy Nick. The crushing loss dropped The Red Army to 8–7 and eliminated them from playoff contention for the fifth straight season. Despite the disappointment, they rebounded in the Toes Bowl bracket, capturing their record fourth Toes Bowl championship and finishing the season 10–7 in fifth place.

The 2023 season saw The Red Army attempt to build on their late-season success they had in 2022, but familiar issues resurfaced. Poor drafting once again buried the team early, especially after first overall pick Justin Jefferson missed nearly half the season with injury. By rivalry week, The Red Army stood at 5–9 in ninth place, once again staring down the possibility of another Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. Needing a win against rival Sleepy Nick to avoid that fate, The Red Army delivered, riding big performances from Kyren Williams and Matthew Stafford to a crucial victory. The win pushed them to a 6–9 finish and eighth place, safely out of last. They carried that momentum into the Toes Bowl bracket, winning their second straight Toes Bowl championship and ending the season 8–9 in fifth place.

Entering the 2024 season, The Red Army held legitimate hopes of finally returning to the playoffs for the first time since 2017. With yet another first overall pick, they selected Christian McCaffrey, believing he could be the missing piece. Those hopes were dashed almost immediately when McCaffrey suffered an Achilles injury before the team’s first game, sidelining him for most of the season. Without their star running back, consistency proved elusive. By rivalry week, The Red Army sat at 7–7 in eighth place, once again on the outside looking in. In the third-ever Manchester Bowl against Sleepy Nick, a must-win situation, poor performances from Derrick Henry and Gus Edwards sealed another loss. The defeat locked The Red Army into their third straight Toes Bowl appearance. Seeking a third consecutive Toes Bowl title, they instead fell in the first round to the Maryland Marijuanas, finishing the season 7–10 in eighth place.

The 2025 season represented another attempt to finally break the playoff drought. This time, The Red Army’s first-round pick did not bust, as they selected Bijan Robinson. With a healthier and more balanced roster, they remained competitive throughout the season. By rivalry week, The Red Army stood 8–6 in fourth place, firmly back in the playoff hunt. In the fourth-ever Manchester Bowl against Sleepy Nick, a win would have clinched their postseason return. Instead, a low-scoring, hard-fought loss—highlighted by poor performances from Ladd McConkey and Jerry Jeudy, once again ended their hopes. The loss dropped them to 8–7 and extended their playoff drought to eight straight seasons. Back in the Toes Bowl bracket, The Red Army fell in the championship game and closed the year 9–8 in sixth place, still searching for a long-awaited breakthrough.
