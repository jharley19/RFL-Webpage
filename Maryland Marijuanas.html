Biography

The Maryland Marijuanas entered the RFL in 2016 and immediately made their presence felt with one of the most impressive debut seasons in league history. In their inaugural campaign, the team finished the regular season at 11–3, good for second place, powered by the dominant Aaron Rodgers–Jordy Nelson stack. However, that regular-season success failed to carry over into the playoffs. Two underwhelming postseason performances saw the Maryland Marijuanas stumble to a fourth-place finish, planting the first seeds of what would become a recurring playoff struggle.

That postseason collapse carried over — and then some — into the 2017 season, which quickly unraveled into one of the worst years ever recorded in league history. The Maryland Marijuanas failed to secure a single win until Week 12, spending most of the season as an automatic victory on opponents’ schedules. They ultimately finished 1–13, dead last in the standings, and firmly established themselves as the league’s primary laughingstock.

Hoping to reverse course in 2018, the Maryland Marijuanas returned with renewed optimism but familiar decision-making. Once again placing their faith in Drew Brees, the team continued to struggle with consistency. The season ended with a 6–8 record, followed by two postseason losses that dropped them to an eighth-place finish, confirming that the franchise remained stuck in mediocrity.

Entering the 2019 season, the Maryland Marijuanas acknowledged that drastic change was necessary. In response, they fully embraced a bizarre and controversial new drafting philosophy dubbed the “No Plan Plan.” Under this strategy, the drafter conducted minimal research and focused almost exclusively on selecting Caucasian wide receivers. While the approach was widely mocked and made little logical sense, it somehow worked. The Maryland Marijuanas rediscovered their early success, finishing the regular season at 10–4 and once again securing second place. The postseason finally brought a breakthrough, as the Maryland Marijuanas earned their first-ever playoff victory with a narrow win over Goofy God. That victory propelled them into their first championship appearance, though the magic ended there, as they were defeated by nearly 40 points in the title game. Despite the lopsided loss, the season was widely regarded as a success, and the Maryland Marijuanas were fittingly honored as the league’s Comeback Player of the Year, completing one of the most improbable turnarounds in RFL history.

Despite failing to achieve their desired outcome the previous year, the Maryland Marijuanas entered the 2020 season committed to the same unconventional strategy that had revived their franchise in 2019. In an effort to strengthen leadership, they briefly brought on Ben Adams as a co-manager. However, the partnership was short-lived, as fundamental disagreements over the team’s long-term vision led to Ben’s release early in the season. The defining moment of the Maryland Marijuanas’ 2020 campaign came in the form of a franchise-altering trade with Gruden’s Plan, a deal that would go down as one of the most lopsided transactions in league history, and not in their favor. Allowing their obsession with white wide receivers and their lingering loyalty to Drew Brees to cloud their judgment, the Maryland Marijuanas traded away Patrick Mahomes, Alvin Kamara, DeSean Jackson, and a first-round Waiver Wire Draft pick in exchange for Drew Brees, Adam Thielen, Todd Gurley, and Zach Ertz. Remarkably, despite the damage caused by the trade, the Maryland Marijuanas still managed to reach the playoffs, finishing the regular season 8–6 in third place. Ironically, their postseason run was cut short in the first round by the very stars they had traded away, and they ultimately ended the year in third place, closing the book on a frustrating and self-inflicted campaign.

Entering the 2021 season, the Maryland Marijuanas were determined to redeem themselves and erase the embarrassment of 2020. They immediately made their statement on draft day, delivering what many consider the best draft in league history, selecting 11 players who finished among the top at their respective positions. The results were immediate and overwhelming. The Maryland Marijuanas won six of their first eight matchups and went on to score the most points in a single season in RFL history. That dominant regular season earned them the #1 seed heading into the playoffs, where they were tasked with facing The Dictator in the first round. Despite their historic season, the Maryland Marijuanas once again fell victim to postseason heartbreak, losing a tightly contested matchup to the league’s most seasoned playoff competitor. For the third consecutive year, they exited the playoffs without a championship, cementing a growing narrative that regular-season dominance had yet to translate into postseason glory.

The 2022 season saw the Maryland Marijuanas pick up right where they left off, continuing their trend of regular-season dominance. Through nine weeks, the team sat at 7–2, highlighted by a historic performance from RB Joe Mixon, who exploded for 55.10 points, setting a new league record for most points scored in a single week. However, after reaching that high point, the season unraveled quickly. The Marijuanas went on to lose five straight games, finding themselves eliminated from playoff contention by Week 15. Despite the late-season collapse, they still managed to defeat rival Mullet Man in the inaugural Crown Bowl, earning a fifth of Crown Royal whiskey for their efforts. The Marijuanas then entered the Toes Bowl, defeating Big Ounce behind a massive 27.30 point performance from RB Saquon Barkley. That victory advanced them to their first Toes Bowl Finals, where they faced The Red Army. The matchup proved lopsided, as the Marijuanas were defeated by 60.30 points, undone by disappointing performances from WR DK Metcalf and WR Terry McLaurin. They finished the season 8–7, good for fifth place.

The 2023 season featured yet another strong regular-season showing from the Maryland Marijuanas. Entering Rivalry Week in Week 15, they held a 9–5 record and were firmly entrenched in the playoff picture. The second edition of the Crown Bowl became one of the most memorable games in league history, as the Marijuanas posted a league record 223.58 points to utterly dismantle rival Mullet Man. The explosion was led by RB Christian McCaffrey and RB James Cook, who combined for 77.80 points. Unfortunately, the momentum came to a halt once the playoffs began. In the first round, the Marijuanas were eliminated by Gruden’s Plan, largely due to a disastrous outing from MVP candidate QB Brock Purdy, who scored –1.60 points against the Ravens defense. The loss marked yet another first round exit, and the Marijuanas closed the season 10–7 in fourth place.

The 2024 season proved more challenging by the Marijuanas’ lofty standards. Entering Rivalry Week in Week 15, they sat at 7–7, needing both a win and outside help to qualify for the playoffs. In the third edition of the Crown Bowl, the Marijuanas once again dominated rival Mullet Man, capturing their third consecutive Crown Bowl victory behind strong performances from RB Jahmyr Gibbs, WR Terry McLaurin, and RB James Cook. While the Marijuanas took care of business, their playoff hopes hinged on Gruden’s Plan securing a win. Instead, a zero-point performance from WR Cooper Kupp and TE T.J. Hockenson failing to make one final catch resulted in a heartbreaking loss by less than a point, officially eliminating the Marijuanas from postseason contention. Despite missing the playoffs, the Marijuanas finished the year on a high note in the Toes Bowl, delivering back-to-back 150+ point performances. Led by QB Joe Burrow and RB Jahmyr Gibbs, they captured their first-ever Toes Bowl Championship, securing the first overall pick in the 2025 draft and closing the season with momentum and renewed optimism for the future.

Armed with the first overall pick, the Maryland Marijuanas entered the 2025 season with renewed confidence and a clear path back to championship contention. Despite the high expectations, consistency proved elusive early in the season, and by Week 13 the Marijuanas found themselves sitting at 6–6, hovering on the edge of the playoff picture. The team surged at the perfect time, winning back-to-back games to set up a win-and-in scenario heading into Rivalry Week in Week 15. In the fourth edition of the Crown Bowl, the Marijuanas once again faced their longtime rival Mullet Man, who entered the matchup in first place and had dominated the league throughout the season. Defying all expectations, the Marijuanas delivered a stunning upset, powered by a truly unlikely and historic performance from TE Kyle Pitts, who erupted for 45.60 points. The victory marked the Marijuanas’ fourth consecutive Crown Bowl win and secured them the final playoff spot. Fate immediately dealt another showdown with Mullet Man in the first round of the playoffs. This time, the battle was far tighter, but strong performances from QB Drake Maye and RB James Cook carried the Marijuanas to a hard-fought 8.04 point victory, sending them to the championship game for the first time since 2019. In the title matchup, the Maryland Marijuanas squared off against Sleepy Nick, and the championship was effectively decided early. QB Drake Maye delivered a dominant 42.44 point performance, setting the tone from the opening slate and leaving little doubt about the outcome. The Marijuanas never relinquished control, cruising to victory and securing the first championship in franchise history, completing one of the most satisfying redemption arcs the league has ever seen.
