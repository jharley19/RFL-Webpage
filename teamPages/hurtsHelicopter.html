Biography

The Hurts Helicopter entered the league in 2017 and immediately made history, though not in a good way. In an unprecedented move, the team slept through the entire draft, which had started in the afternoon. Because the league does not approve of auto-drafting, the Hurts Helicopter was left scraping together a roster of leftovers. Unsurprisingly, this group of misfits struggled, finishing the regular season 7–7 in 6th place. They narrowly avoided disaster in the Loser’s Bracket, ultimately finishing 7th and escaping last place.

Heading into 2018, the Hurts Helicopter was at least awake for the draft and able to build its own roster for the first time. Unfortunately, the results were familiar. The team once again finished the regular season 7–7, landing in 6th place. This time, however, they showed up in the postseason and earned the first overall pick in the 2019 draft.

Armed with the top pick in 2019, the Hurts Helicopter made another major addition by bringing on close friend Nick Sagun as co-manager. Unfortunately, Nick arrived late to the draft and was unable to stop the team from selecting Le’Veon Bell with the first overall pick. A disappointing draft led to an even more disappointing season. After winning their opening game, the Hurts Helicopter lost eight straight, finishing 3–11 and dead last. They then fell to The Red Army in the inaugural Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, earning the league’s first-ever sanctioned punishment: performing the league national anthem at the start of the 2020 draft and serving as the draft’s “sticker bitches.”

Entering 2020, Nick Sagun departed to run his own team, and the Hurts Helicopter set out on a revenge tour following their humiliating punishment. The response was emphatic. They started the season 3–1, then rattled off nine straight wins, finishing the regular season 12–2 in first place. A legitimate wagon, the Hurts Helicopter cruised into their first playoff appearance. They narrowly defeated their former co-manager’s new team in the semifinals, advancing to their first championship game. Despite a strong effort, they fell to Gruden’s Plan, finishing as league runner-up.

In 2021, the franchise entered a new era. With the Eagles moving on from Carson Wentz, the team followed suit, officially rebranding from The Wentz Wagon to The Hurts Helicopter. The change paid immediate dividends. A strong draft landed Jonathan Taylor in the second round and Justin Jefferson in the third. The Hurts Helicopter battled all season and entered the final week 7–7, sitting in 4th place in a must-win matchup against Mullet Man. Behind a dominant performance from Taylor, they secured the win and clinched a playoff berth. In the first round, they defeated Big Ounce in a high-scoring thriller thanks to Dak Prescott’s 39.30 point performance. In the championship against The Dictator, Prescott delivered again, lifting the Hurts Helicopter to their first RFL championship.

Looking to defend their title in 2022, the Hurts Helicopter avoided any championship hangover. They crushed the draft, selecting eight top players, including the biggest steal of the year in Tony Pollard in the seventh round. After an uneven 4–4 start, the team made a bold, season-altering trade, sending Tyreek Hill and Josh Jacobs (along with other pieces) to Mullet Man in exchange for Christian McCaffrey and Davante Adams (along with other pieces). The move transformed the roster. By the final week, the Hurts Helicopter sat 8–6 in 2nd place and were widely viewed as the most dangerous team in the league. They dominated Goofy God in the inaugural rivalry week matchup, highlighted by a 36.74 point performance from Patrick Mahomes, and returned to the playoffs. In the first round, they faced Sleepy Nick again, whose owner had once been on their staff back in 2019. In heartbreaking fashion, the Hurts Helicopter lost by 1.84 points, undone by a –4-point performance from the Broncos defense. Despite the disappointment, they entered 2023 with momentum and one of the strongest rosters in league history.

The 2023 season, however, marked a sharp downturn. The Hurts Helicopter lost seven of their first nine games, and by rivalry week sat 5–9 in 8th place, eliminated from playoff contention for the first time since 2019. A win was still needed to avoid the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, but they fell to Goofy God, thanks to poor performances from Saquon Barkley and DeAndre Hopkins. Sent to the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl for the first time since 2019, they faced Big Ounce and suffered another defeat after a disastrous second week, highlighted by a goose from Cole Kmet. The Hurts Helicopter finished last place and were forced to endure their first beer mile.

Determined to rebound in 2024, the Hurts Helicopter returned with renewed focus following their last-place finish and embarrassing beer mile performance. While no longer the offensive juggernaut of previous seasons, they found ways to win and stayed competitive throughout the year. By rivalry week, they were 8–6 in 5th place, needing a win to reach the playoffs. Despite a strong scoring effort, duds from Jake Ferguson and David Montgomery led to a narrow loss against Goofy God, eliminating them from postseason contention for the second straight year. In their first Toes Bowl appearance since 2018, they fell to The Dictator after poor outings from Justice Hill and Jerry Jeudy, finishing 7th.

In 2025, the Hurts Helicopter aimed to return to relevance. They started strong, and by rivalry week sat 8–6 in 3rd place, needing one win against Goofy God to clinch a playoff spot. In a high-scoring showdown, they exploded for 176.52 points, fueled by massive performances from Jared Goff, Trey McBride, and TreVeyon Henderson, securing their first playoff berth since 2022. In the first round, they faced Sleepy Nick, who had eliminated them in 2022. Unfortunately, history repeated itself, as disappointing games from McBride and Henderson led to another heartbreaking playoff loss. The Hurts Helicopter finished 4th, closing the season with renewed optimism after finally returning to the postseason.
