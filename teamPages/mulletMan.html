Biography

The Mullet Man entered the RFL in 2018 and immediately discovered just how unforgiving the league could be. Their inaugural draft proved disastrous, as they assembled a clearly below-average roster and even executed a baffling pick swap with Gruden’s Plan that yielded nothing in return beyond extra time to consider their selection. The results were immediate and brutal. The Mullet Man opened the season with five straight losses and limped to a 2–12 record, finishing ninth. The season served as a harsh introduction to league life and quickly dispelled any notion that success would come easily.

Heading into the 2019 season, The Mullet Man adopted an unconventional strategy, becoming the only team in the league to forgo selecting a keeper. While the decision raised eyebrows across the league, it ultimately paid dividends. The Mullet Man delivered the best season in franchise history, finishing the regular season 7–7 and narrowly missing the playoffs in fifth place. They carried that momentum into the Toes Bowl, winning their next two games and securing the first overall pick in the 2020 draft.

With the top pick in hand entering 2020, The Mullet Man had long since decided their selection would be Christian McCaffrey, the star running back from their favorite NFL team. Prior to the draft, they also brought in recently released Ben Adams as a co-manager. Draft night, however, raised plenty of concerns around the league. Questionable selections included George Kittle in the second round, T.Y. Hilton in the fourth, relatively unknown rookie Antonio Gibson in the ninth, and the controversial pick of recently released Derrius Guice in the fifteenth round. When McCaffrey suffered an early-season injury, the team’s already shaky foundation collapsed. In an effort to salvage the season, The Mullet Man became hyperactive on the trade market, completing a league record eight trades in a single season. Despite the flurry of moves, the results failed to materialize. The team missed the playoffs for the third consecutive year, finishing the regular season 6–8 in sixth place and ultimately ending the year seventh overall. To this day, The Mullet Man remains the only non-rookie franchise in the league to never make the playoffs.

Entering the 2021 season, co-manager Ben Adams found himself at a crossroads. He emerged as a candidate for franchise ownership elsewhere in the league but failed to pass his interview process. Following that setback, Ben appeared disengaged in his role with The Mullet Man and explored opportunities on the open market. He showed particular interest in joining Sleepy Nick, believing he would have greater influence over roster decisions, but the team declined to add additional consultation. With no alternatives materializing, Ben ultimately re-signed with The Mullet Man, keeping the partnership intact as the franchise continued its search for relevance. This season the team performed much better, and by the final week of the regular season, they were in third place at 8-6 and needed just to win to secure a playoff spot. However, a loss to The Hurts Helicopter in week 15, thanks to poor performances by RB Cordarelle Patterson, WR Rashod Bateman, and WR Brandon Aiyuk, would once again eliminate Mullet Man from playoff contention, ending the regular season in fifth. In the Toes Bowl, the Mullet Man got back in to their winning ways, defeating Gruden's Plan and then Sleepy Nick to secure their second Toes Bowl championship and the first overall pick for the 2022 draft.

Entering the 2022 season, co-manager Ben Adams and The Mullet Man mutually agreed to part ways, with Ben stepping down from his role. In his place, the team hired Alan Ashford, brother of Mullet Man owner Billy Goodnow, as the new co-manager. The move was seen as a potential turning point that could finally push The Mullet Man into playoff contention. Unfortunately, the partnership got off to a rocky start. Despite holding the first overall pick and selecting RB Christian McCaffrey, the draft quickly unraveled. Third and fourth round picks Javonte Williams and J.K. Dobbins, who were expected to anchor the running back room, both suffered major injuries that sidelined them for most of the season. Despite the crumbling foundation, The Mullet Man leaned back into their familiar strategy of hyperactivity on the trade market, completing seven trades during the season. One deal saw them trade away Javonte Williams just one week before he tore his ACL. Another blockbuster brought in RB Najee Harris and TE T.J. Hockenson in exchange for WR Curtis Samuel and TE Hayden Hurst, a trade that would win biggest trade rape of the year. These aggressive moves paid dividends, as the team surged to 8–4 and second place by Week 13. However, the momentum didn’t last. The Mullet Man dropped their final three regular-season games, including a loss to the Maryland Marijuanas in the inaugural Crown Bowl, finishing 8–7. Despite the collapse, they squeaked into the playoffs as the 4th seed, marking the first postseason appearance in franchise history. In the opening round, even a 35.90 point performance from T.J. Hockenson wasn’t enough, as they fell to The Dictator, ending their playoff run and finishing fourth overall.

Looking ahead to the 2023 season, The Mullet Man aimed to build on their breakthrough year. Despite the progress, the team once again parted ways with Alan Ashford and made the questionable decision to bring back former co-manager Ben Adams, hoping familiarity and experience could take them further. The gamble appeared to work. By Rivalry Week in Week 15, The Mullet Man sat in third place at 8–6, needing just one win to secure a playoff berth. In the second edition of the Crown Bowl against rival Maryland Marijuanas, elite performances from QB Jared Goff and TE Sam LaPorta weren’t enough to overcome Maryland’s historic 223.58 point outburst. With their playoff hopes hanging by a thread, The Mullet Man needed help elsewhere. Thanks to The Red Army defeating Sleepy Nick, they clinched a postseason spot, finishing 8–7 and fourth place. In the first round of the playoffs, The Mullet Man faced top-seeded Goofy God. Powered by a massive 43.10 point performance from RB Breece Hall, they finally earned their first playoff victory and advanced to the championship game. There, they faced Gruden’s Plan. Behind another huge showing from Breece Hall and a dominant 40.20 point performance from first round pick WR CeeDee Lamb, The Mullet Man completed their improbable rise by capturing their first RFL Championship.

The 2024 season began with the pressure of defending a title, but chaos quickly followed. In a shocking turn, Ben Adams once again departed from the team. The Mullet Man turned back to Alan Ashford, who agreed to return as co-manager. Despite renewed optimism, the team appeared to suffer from a classic championship hangover. By Week 15, they sat at 5–9, ninth place, and firmly entrenched in the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. Their struggles continued in the third Crown Bowl against the Maryland Marijuanas, suffering a third straight rivalry loss after zero point performances from WR Amari Cooper and WR Darnell Mooney. The season came down to survival in their first ever Panzerkampfwagen Bowl matchup against Sleepy Nick, where two massive games from RB Jonathan Taylor allowed The Mullet Man to narrowly avoid the dreaded beer mile, finishing ninth overall.

Entering the 2025 season, The Mullet Man sought redemption after flirting with disaster the year prior. With Alan Ashford still in place, the team focused on reloading through the draft and smart roster construction. The approach paid off immediately, highlighted by standout selections WR Jaxon Smith-Njigba and RB Jahmyr Gibbs. The Mullet Man dominated the regular season, sitting in first place at 12–1 heading into Rivalry Week. In the fourth edition of the Crown Bowl, however, a rare dud from QB Joe Burrow resulted in yet another loss to the Maryland Marijuanas, marking their fourth consecutive Crown Bowl defeat. The rivalry carried straight into the playoffs, where The Mullet Man sought revenge in a high-stakes rematch. Once again, the Maryland Marijuanas prevailed, ending The Mullet Man’s title defense. Despite the disappointing finish, The Mullet Man closed the season third overall, reinforcing their status as one of the league’s most volatile — yet dangerous — franchises.
