History

The RFL was founded in 2013 by commissioner Jeremy Harley. Having endured several disappointing fantasy football experiences marked by inactive commissioners and disengaged managers, Jeremy set out to create a league that would be both competitive and active. However, assembling a group of quality managers proved more difficult than expected. As a result, the league’s inaugural season featured only six teams — though, in truth, just five unique managers. Jeremy brought on childhood friends Brian Levesque, Zachary Przybocki, and Ben Martin, along with his brother Brandon Harley. That left the league one team short, forcing Jeremy to create a second team of his own just to meet the minimum requirement. Despite its humble beginnings, the pilot season was a success. The managers had fun, competition was lively, and Jeremy’s dream of building a lasting league was alive. With that, the RFL earned its renewal for another season.

Heading into 2014, Jeremy set his sights on expanding the league. Determined to find more dedicated players, he increased his recruiting efforts significantly. His work paid off, and the league grew from five participants to eight, welcoming four new faces: Jonathan Heng, Nick Sagun, Justin Ferguson, and Sanaya Clark — notably, the first and only female manager in league history. Meanwhile, Brandon Harley parted ways with the league after the 2013 season, perhaps still reeling from his heartbreaking championship loss. While the league had successfully grown in size, enthusiasm and engagement proved to be a challenge. Several new managers were inactive, and one never even set a lineup. Though the expansion brought excitement, it was clear that major changes were needed to make the league truly competitive.

As the 2015 season approached, commissioner Harley took decisive action to address inactivity. Two members — Jonathan Heng and Justin Ferguson — were removed from the league for their lack of participation. Once again, Jeremy was back on the recruiting trail, determined to find active and passionate replacements. This time, his search led him to two fellow bandmates and clarinetists: Brian Thomas and Adam Cullen. The new recruits brought immediate success, with both making the playoffs in their debut season. Unfortunately, some of the previous year’s additions — Sanaya Clark and Nick Sagun — failed to set a lineup all season, resulting in another year of uneven competition. While 2015 showed progress in recruitment, the league still had work to do to achieve the level of competitiveness and engagement Jeremy envisioned.

The 2016 season marked a pivotal moment in the evolution of the RFL. This year brought sweeping changes as Commissioner Jeremy Harley entered high school, made new friends, and inevitably left some old ones behind. Several roster changes shook up the league. Sanaya Clark was removed for inactivity, while Brian Thomas was excluded after transferring to a different high school. Nick Sagun, who had been inactive the previous season, was initially set to return out of sheer necessity — but backed out at the last moment. His abandoned team was ultimately managed by the commissioner himself. To fill the remaining vacancies, Jeremy recruited two new friends: Harry Heck and Tyler Ulmschneider. The additions proved immediately impactful — both newcomers made the playoffs, with Tyler capping off his debut season by winning the championship. Their early success signaled a new wave of committed and competitive managers who would shape the league’s future for years to come.

The 2017 season is often regarded as the official beginning of the league’s “New Era.” It featured the largest expansion in league history, bringing in four new members and redefining the league’s identity. Departing were Adam Cullen (due to inactivity), Zachary Przybocki (who had moved away), Ben Martin (who had grown distant), and Nick Sagun (not invited back). To replace them, Jeremy welcomed a new class of managers: RC Miller, a soccer friend; Michael Boswell Jr. and Zane Lehosky, new high school friends; and the returning Brandon Harley. This season is remembered as one of the most significant in league history — the year when things truly started to get serious. Rivalries intensified, competitiveness soared, and the league’s culture began to take shape. The 2017 season also gave rise to one of the most infamous events in league lore: the “Fabiano Trade.” In a move that would go down in history, Gruden’s Plan (managed by Michael Boswell Jr.) traded Tom Brady and LeSean McCoy to The Dictator in exchange for Marcus Mariota and Ty Montgomery. The trade was so shocking that it was submitted to NFL Fantasy Live via Twitter — and made it onto the show, where analyst Michael Fabiano publicly roasted Boswell for the disastrous deal. Adding to the drama, the season ended with The Dictator celebrating their championship victory in the league’s first-ever team video, cementing 2017 as the year that the league truly came alive.

The 2018 season marked a milestone in the history of the RFL, symbolizing the league’s transition from a casual group of friends to a fully established, competitive organization. For the first time ever, no teams were lost between seasons — a testament to the growing commitment of its members. In fact, the league expanded to a full 10-team format, welcoming two new high school friends: Jason Rowland and Billy Goodnow. This season also brought several landmark achievements that elevated the league’s professionalism and prestige. For the first time, the league invested in a purchased draft board and introduced a championship trophy, both of which were unveiled during the inaugural awards ceremony. Another major step forward was the creation of the league’s first official constitution — a written document consolidating all rules and regulations in one place. Among the new provisions introduced were the Keeper System, allowing each team to retain one player from the previous season’s roster in exchange for forfeiting a draft pick equivalent to that player’s average draft position. Half-PPR Scoring Format was also introduced. With these innovations, the 2018 season represented a turning point. The league had evolved into something truly special — a committed, growing community where the competition was fierce and the camaraderie even stronger. It was clear that what Jeremy Harley started back in 2013 had transformed into something far greater than he ever imagined.

The 2019 season was a landmark year for the RFL, representing a creative explosion of innovation, structure, and personality t hat would permanently shape the league’s identity. Before the season began, Tyler Ulmschneider was removed from the league, as it became clear that he didn’t quite fit the core dynamic of the group. In his place, Commissioner Jeremy Harley recruited another high school friend, Logan Handwork, who quickly became a valuable addition. This season ushered in numerous new traditions and systems that defined the modern era of the league. It began with the inaugural Annual Settings Summit (ASS) — a preseason meeting where members debated and voted on potential additions or amendments to the league’s constitution. One of the most important developments to come out of the 2019 ASS was the creation of the FRC — a commissioner-appointed body designed to advise the Commissioner and oversee trades to ensure fairness and prevent collusion. Other major rule changes and innovations from the 2019 season included: the introduction of waivers, replacing the previous free-for-all system, and the removal of Kickers, replaced by a second FLEX spot, adding more strategic depth to roster management. It was also the first season for a League Punishment for Last Place, and the debut of the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, an intense two-week matchup between the 9th and 10th place teams to determine who would endure the punishment. The league also entered a media revolution in 2019. For the first time, draft day confessionals were introduced, short video clips of managers sharing their live reactions, strategies, and emotions during the draft. These were later compiled into what became an award-winning video, setting a new creative benchmark for the league. This year also saw The Dictator raise the bar for team branding and entertainment. They released the first-ever team name announcement video, followed by an unprecedented run of weekly diss tracks, one for every other team in the league. The creativity and humor of these videos made them instant classics and cemented The Dictator as a cultural force within the league. Building on this momentum, Commissioner Harley launched the first weekly recap and preview show, where he and guest analysts discussed matchups, predicted winners, and offered “lock and upset” picks for NFL games. Though the show was discontinued after the season ended, it became a beloved part of league history. Finally, the annual awards ceremony received a major upgrade with the introduction of exit interviews. These segments gave owners a chance to reflect on their seasons, address their draft day comments, and outline their plans for the following year — often with a mix of humor, honesty, and friendly roasting. By the end of 2019, the RFL had truly come into its own — not just as a fantasy competition, but as a creative, connected, and vibrant community.

Leading up to the 2020 season, the league underwent another round of significant changes that would further professionalize its operations and expand its already rich culture. Logan Handwork was released from the league due to a lack of participation, as school and work commitments began to weigh heavily on his schedule. However, his involvement in the league wasn’t over as Logan was immediately added to Gruden’s Plan as a co-manager, marking the first time in league history that a former owner transitioned into a co-manager role. To fill the newly vacated spot, an old name resurfaced: Nick Sagun. After serving as The Hurts Helicopter’s co-manager in 2019, Nick had developed a genuine interest in the modern incarnation of the league. Yet, his return was not guaranteed. Given his checkered history of inactivity in the early years, skepticism surrounded his candidacy. To ensure fairness and accountability, the league established a new entry protocol during its first in-person Annual Settings Summit (ASS) — the introduction of The Inclusion Tribunal (TIT), a formal interview process conducted jointly by the Commissioner and the FRC. This marked a symbolic shift from the early years of begging people to join to now requiring formal interviews to earn admission. The 2020 ASS also brought several other monumental changes, including the introduction of monetary stakes for the first time and the creation of the Waiver Wire Draft, adding yet another layer of strategy to roster management. Nick became the first-ever candidate to undergo a TIT interview, which he successfully passed, officially rejoining the league as a full-time manager. The offseason also saw the rise of team press conferences, used to announce major organizational changes such as hiring or firing co-managers and introducing new members. Additionally, team name announcement videos became a cultural phenomenon, with memorable releases from Babylon, The Dictator, The Red Army, Sleepy Nick, and Goofy God. The 2020 Draft set a new standard for league events. For the first time, 100% of managers attended in person, and the draft was held outdoors, marking another league first. The festivities began with the presentation of the league’s national anthem, performed by the inaugural Panzerkampfwagen Bowl losers, Zane Lehosky and Nick Sagun. Once the season began, The Dictator once again took the spotlight with a new series of weekly propaganda videos titled “7 Minutes of Hate.” These segments featured The Dictator’s commentary on major league storylines and updates on their team’s performance, further cementing their reputation as the league’s creative powerhouse. At the 2020 Awards Ceremony, the league’s signature blend of humor and accountability was on full display, as several water bets were paid out and The Red Army received their pie-in-the-face punishment. The 2020 season was a defining year — a perfect blend of structure, creativity, and camaraderie that showcased just how far the RFL had come from its humble six-team beginnings.

Entering the 2021 season, the league faced one of its most dramatic off-field controversies to date. A petition was created and signed by nine league members, calling for the removal of Babylon from the RFL. The owner of Babylon, RC Miller, had gradually grown apart from the league, both socially and culturally. His repeated antics had worn thin, and it became clear to many that he no longer fit the dynamic of the group. After careful consideration, the Commissioner approved the petition, officially removing Babylon from the league. With a vacant spot open, the Commissioner once again convened The Inclusion Tribunal (TIT) to find a worthy replacement. Three initial candidates were identified: Ben Adams, co-manager of Mullet Man; Alex Garcia, co-manager of Big Ounce; and Adam Fenton, a friend of the league. Ben and Alex completed their interviews, but Adam withdrew at the last minute after failing to respond to the Commissioner’s messages. None of the candidates had yet convinced the Tribunal to make an offer. In a last-minute effort to find the right fit, Michael Boswell Jr. recommended a friend — Zach Zalovick. Demonstrating remarkable commitment, Zach left a wedding early and even postponed a booty call just to complete his interview. His enthusiasm and charisma impressed the panel immediately. Zach was offered the position and officially became the 10th member of the RFL for the 2021 season. Meanwhile, The Dictator once again pushed the boundaries of league creativity, debuting a groundbreaking video series chronicling their entire offseason and preparation for the 2021 campaign. The series was unlike anything the league had seen before — cinematic, detailed, and ambitious — and set a new standard for storytelling and media in the RFL. In another major milestone, the Commissioner began developing the first-ever official RFL website — the very platform you’re reading this on now. The site was envisioned as the central hub for all things RFL: a living archive of league history, statistics, and media. With new technology, fresh faces, and unprecedented creativity, the 2021 season stood as a testament to just how far the league had evolved from its humble origins — transforming into not just a fantasy football competition, but a thriving digital community and multimedia experience.

The 2022 season marked the 10th annual campaign of the RFL, and it proved to be one of the most historic seasons in league history. For the first time ever, the same set of managers returned for back-to-back seasons — a testament to the league’s growing stability and dedication among its members. To commemorate this milestone, the league held its first ever destination draft in Ocean City, Maryland. Though the managers were packed tightly into a humble beach house, the weekend was nothing short of legendary. Between draft rounds, members enjoyed the beach, the boardwalk, and a healthy dose of drinking games, creating memories that would define the league’s tenth year. The 2022 season also saw the debut of The Dictator’s most ambitious project to date — a full-length video series chronicling their offseason journey. The highlight of the series was a trip to Dictation Island, a reality TV–style saga exploring the relationship between The Dictator and his beloved fantasy goddess, Scabby Katie. The production quality and storytelling elevated league media to new heights, blurring the line between fantasy football and entertainment. Throughout the season, The Dictator continued to deliver weekly videos previewing upcoming matchups. Each week featured a “main event”, pairing two teams in a cinematic showdown complete with dramatic promos and trash talk. These videos became a cornerstone of league culture, generating excitement and engagement across the board. This season also introduced the first official Rivalry Week, a special event where every matchup was determined by historical rivalries within the league. Each rivalry bowl came with its own custom punishment, raising the stakes even higher. For the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, the league elevated the punishment once again, instituting the now-infamous Beer Mile, a grueling and humiliating test that would become one of the league’s most feared traditions. Finally, 2022 marked another key gameplay change: the introduction of full PPR scoring, further modernizing the league’s competitive format. This year would also be historic as it introduced a new league format: dynasty. In addition to the traditional redraft league, a parallel dynasty league was established, allowing managers to build long-term rosters and strategies. This expansion added a new layer of depth and complexity to the RFL experience. The 10th season of the RFL wasn’t just a celebration of endurance — it was a showcase of creativity, brotherhood, and the sheer absurd brilliance that made this league the greatest fantasy football league of all time.

The 2023 RFL season built on the momentum and traditions of previous years while introducing new elements to keep the league fresh and engaging. The season kicked off with a destination draft at HYCO Lake, North Carolina, a weekend packed with memorable moments: an epic wiffle ball showdown, the infamous “bad bitches draft,” and, for Brandon Harley, an unfortunate amount of work meetings. Hurts Helicopter owner Zane Lehosky had one of the weekend’s most notorious moments when he yelled at Brandon, “Why don’t you meet some bitches?” The trip was capped off by Gruden’s Plan owner Michael Boswell Jr. running the league’s first-ever beer mile, delivering one of the most hilarious moments in RFL history. Although weekly videos were retired this season and The Dictator remained absent, the league continued to thrive both on and off the field.

The 2024 RFL season continued the league’s destination draft tradition, this time in Lake Norman, North Carolina. The weekend was packed with memorable moments, including water football, the infamous racism rankings debate, and, for some, marathon Madden sessions. The Hurts Helicopter owner Zane Lehosky delivered what might go down as the worst, least entertaining, and brutally unwatchable beer mile performances in league history, leaving many to question whether the punishment was worth it. The trip also featured the first edition of the Dynasty rookie draft, followed by a punishment for Indianapolis Longhorns owner Billy Goodnow: serving as the weekend’s housemaid, cleaning up after everyone, and even preparing a meal for the league to devour. Despite NFL.com issues and the continued absence of The Dictator, the league pushed forward, keeping its competitive spirit and camaraderie alive.

The 2025 RFL season marked a major operational shift. With ongoing issues related to NFL.com, the league made the bold move to Sleeper, aiming to provide a more reliable and user-friendly fantasy football experience. The season kicked off with another destination draft, returning to Lake Norman, North Carolina. The weekend was filled with unforgettable moments, including a heated debate over whether Wawa carried certain groceries, the league’s first-ever golf tournament, and late-night hot tub conversations revealing that Maryland Marijuanas owner Harry Heck was about to become a dad. While The Dictator remained absent and the media presence stayed minimal, the league’s competitive spirit and camaraderie continued as strong as ever.
