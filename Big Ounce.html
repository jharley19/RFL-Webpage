Biography

Big Ounce began its first reign during the league’s inaugural 2013 season, finishing with a strong 10–3 record and earning second place. After a first-round victory, Big Ounce advanced to the league’s first-ever championship match, where they ultimately fell to the top-seeded The Dictator. Following this defeat, Big Ounce went on a four-year hiatus, disappearing from league play until a return in 2017.

In 2017, signs of rust were evident, and Big Ounce narrowly missed the playoffs. Instead of vanishing again after a disappointing comeback, the team chose to regroup and set their sights on redemption in 2018.

The 2018 season marked a triumphant return to form. Big Ounce posted a 10–4 record, once again finishing second overall. They advanced to the championship match to face the top-seeded Cookie Charizards—and this time, Big Ounce emerged victorious, capturing their first league title.

In 2019, Big Ounce aimed to go back-to-back. Teaming up with longtime friend and co-manager Alexis Garcia, and led by elite keeper Christian McCaffrey, the team dominated the regular season with an 11–3 record and the most points scored in the league. Their playoff performance was equally commanding, scoring over 140 points in each matchup to claim a second straight championship—the first franchise in league history to accomplish the back-to-back feat.

The 2020 season brought aspirations of a historic three-peat, but injuries and a shaky draft—along with the loss of star player Christian McCaffrey—derailed those hopes. After spending much of the season in last place, Big Ounce clawed their way out of the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl but ultimately finished 8th overall.

The 2021 season offered a chance at redemption after a disappointing 2020 campaign. Despite another challenging draft and a string of injuries—including a season-ending one to Derrick Henry, who had been carrying the team early on, Big Ounce persevered to finish the regular season in 2nd place. In the playoffs, they fell in the first round to eventual champion The Hurts Helicopter despite putting up an impressive 139.88 points, ultimately securing a 4th-place finish.

The 2022 season was a mediocre one for Big Ounce, finishing with an 8–7 record and missing the playoffs. One bright spot was the midseason signing of undrafted free agent RB Jerick McKinnon of the Chiefs, who took over the backfield by Week 13 and became a valuable late-season asset. However, in Week 15, Big Ounce fell to The Dictator in the inaugural Mario Bowl, forcing them to pay their dues by mowing the entire lawn of his dad’s house. In the Toes Bowl, Big Ounce lost in the first round to the Maryland Marijuanas and ultimately finished 7th overall.

The 2023 season was one to forget. With the third overall pick, Big Ounce selected RB Austin Ekeler, who was injured in Week 1 and placed on IR. After returning in Week 6, he never regained form, finishing with just six touchdowns and earning the title of Biggest Draft Bust of the Season. Despite the setback, Big Ounce stayed competitive, sitting at 5–5 and in fifth place by Week 10. Unfortunately, a five-game losing streak, including another Mario Bowl loss to The Dictator, sent them plummeting to last place. They were relegated to the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, where they managed a win over The Hurts Helicopter, finishing 9th overall.

The 2024 season marked a massive comeback for Big Ounce. After a 5–5 start, the team went on a historic scoring run, topping 150 points in five of their last six games, including a team-record 217.92 points in Week 14 against The Hurts Helicopter. Still sitting at 7–7 after Week 14, Big Ounce needed a win (and some help) to make the playoffs. In Week 15, they earned their first-ever Mario Bowl victory over The Dictator, fueled by QB Jared Goff’s five-touchdown, 51.06-point explosion. That win pushed Big Ounce into the playoffs as the 4th seed via tiebreaker over The Maryland Marijuanas, The Dictator, and The Hurts Helicopter. In the playoffs, Big Ounce stunned the top-seeded Goofy God with a 192.54-point performance to reach their first championship since 2019. However, their magic ran out in the finals, as disappointing showings from James Conner, De’Von Achane, and the Colts D/ST doomed them against The Wack Attacks. Big Ounce finished the season 2nd overall.

The 2025 season brought disappointment after such promise. Despite a dominant Week 1 win, Big Ounce collapsed, losing nine straight to fall to 1–9 and becoming the first team eliminated from playoff contention. Yet, a late-season resurgence led by QB Josh Allen and star WR Puka Nacua sparked hope, as the team won three of its next four games. That set up a dramatic Week 15 showdown in the fourth Mario Bowl against The Dictator. Big Ounce emerged victorious for the second straight year, powered by standout performances from Allen, Nacua, and RB Tyrone Tracy. The win allowed Big Ounce to finish 5–10, narrowly avoiding the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. In the Toes Bowl, they kept the momentum going, defeating Goofy God and then The Red Army in the championship to claim their second Toes Bowl title in franchise history.
