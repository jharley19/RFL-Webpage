Biography

Sleepy Nick first entered the RFL in 2014 and immediately established a reputation for indifference. The team made little effort to improve its roster or compete at a meaningful level, spending minimal time managing lineups or strategy. That lack of commitment was reflected in the standings, as Sleepy Nick finished the season with just two wins, ending the year in seventh place.

Heading into the 2015 season, expectations were low but cautiously optimistic. League experts believed that Sleepy Nick would learn from the previous year’s mistakes and finally begin to take the league seriously. Those expectations proved wildly inaccurate. Instead of improving, Sleepy Nick managed to become even less engaged, failing to set a starting lineup for the entire season. The result was a historically poor campaign, finishing 0–14 and once again landing in seventh place. Following the season, Sleepy Nick was formally removed from the league for negligence, and owner Nick Sagun disappeared from the RFL landscape for nearly four years.

Leading up to the 2020 season, Nick Sagun re-emerged as a candidate to fill an open franchise spot, largely due to his contributions as a co-manager on The Hurts Helicopter during the 2019 season. While some members of the FRC remained skeptical of his renewed commitment given his past failures, the league decided to put him to the test. This led to the first-ever Inclusion Tribunal interview, where Nick’s competence and dedication were thoroughly evaluated. Nick ultimately passed the interview and was officially welcomed back into the RFL. Embracing his reputation, he declared that just as the haters would sleep on him, he would sleep on the haters, a sentiment reinforced by his well-known narcoleptic tendencies. With that, he announced his new team name: Sleepy Nick. True to form, many teams underestimated Sleepy Nick heading into the season. Despite a questionable draft and several head-scratching decisions, the team proved to be remarkably resilient. With an underwhelming roster on paper, Sleepy Nick nonetheless found ways to grind out victories and remain competitive. That resilience paid off, as they managed to sneak into the playoffs for the first time with a modest 7–7 record. However, their postseason run quickly revealed their limitations, as Sleepy Nick lost both playoff games and finished the season in fourth place.

In 2021, Sleepy Nick entered the season with high hopes of building on their surprise playoff appearance the year prior. Those expectations quickly met reality, as the team stumbled out of the gate. After ten weeks, Sleepy Nick sat at 5–5, good for sixth place, and that mediocrity persisted through the remainder of the season. They ultimately finished 7–8, narrowly missing the playoffs and settling into the Toes Bowl bracket. In the first round, Sleepy Nick delivered a dominant performance against the Wack Attacks, powered largely by a massive 37.40 point outing from Tee Higgins. However, their momentum came to a halt in the Toes Bowl final, where a disastrous 3.50 point performance from first-round pick Ezekiel Elliott doomed them in a loss to Mullet Man. Sleepy Nick finished the season in sixth place, earning the second overall pick in the 2022 draft.

Armed with that second overall pick, Sleepy Nick entered 2022 with legitimate championship aspirations, and this time, they delivered. The team remained competitive throughout the season, and by rivalry week in Week 15, they were 8–6, sitting in fifth place and needing a win to secure a playoff berth. In the inaugural Manchester Bowl against The Red Army, Sleepy Nick squeaked out a dramatic 2.04 point victory, thanks to a late Monday Night Football surge from Aaron Jones, clinching their playoff spot. In the first round of the playoffs, Sleepy Nick faced The Hurts Helicopter and survived yet another nail-biter, winning by 1.84 points behind strong performances from Joe Burrow and Tee Higgins. That victory sent Sleepy Nick to their first-ever RFL championship game, where they faced the top-seeded Dictator. Defying the odds once again, Sleepy Nick pulled off the upset on the strength of big games from D.J. Moore and Dalton Schultz, capturing their first RFL championship. The title run cemented their identity as a resilient team capable of thriving in high-pressure moments.

Following their championship triumph, Sleepy Nick entered 2023 with hopes of going back-to-back. The season, however, followed a familiar script early on. Through ten weeks, they once again sat at 5–5 in sixth place. While they showed signs of life late, winning three of their next four games, they entered rivalry week at 8–6 and in fourth place, still needing a win to clinch a playoff berth. In a must-win Manchester Bowl rematch against The Red Army, Sleepy Nick suffered their first loss in the rivalry, undone by disappointing performances from Bijan Robinson and Justin Fields. The defeat knocked them out of playoff contention and sent them to the Toes Bowl. Their struggles continued there, as they lost once again to The Red Army, this time due to duds from Diontae Johnson and Jakobi Meyers. Sleepy Nick finished the season in seventh place, marking a sharp regression from their championship campaign.

Desperate to rebound, Sleepy Nick entered 2024 hoping to return to relevance, but the season quickly unraveled. A historically bad draft crippled the roster, as five of their first six selections delivered disappointing seasons due to injuries or poor performance. Notably, quarterback Anthony Richardson failed to live up to expectations, while Isiah Pacheco suffered a major injury in Week 2. With a flawed foundation, the season spiraled out of control. By Week 11, Sleepy Nick had only two wins and was officially eliminated from playoff contention. Entering rivalry week, they were 3–11 and firmly in last place. In the third edition of the Manchester Bowl against a similarly struggling Red Army, Sleepy Nick managed a rare bright moment, winning behind a 26.60 point performance from CeeDee Lamb, one of the few positives all year. Despite the victory, Sleepy Nick finished the regular season in last place and advanced to their first-ever Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, where they fell to Mullet Man, who delivered a historic Bowl performance. The loss condemned Sleepy Nick to last place and forced them to endure the dreaded beer mile punishment.

Entering 2025, Sleepy Nick was determined to avenge their humiliating finish and reclaim their status as a contender. The draft marked a significant improvement, highlighted by steals such as Jalen Hurts in the sixth round and Jonathan Taylor in the third. However, their top two selections, Justin Jefferson and Brian Thomas Jr., both underperformed, contributing to a sluggish start that saw Sleepy Nick lose three of their first four games. Once again, though, resilience defined the season. The team surged late, and by rivalry week they were 8–6, fighting for a playoff spot. In yet another must-win Manchester Bowl against The Red Army, Sleepy Nick emerged victorious behind a dominant performance from Jalen Hurts, securing their first playoff berth since 2022. In the opening round, they faced The Hurts Helicopter and delivered a statement win, fueled by a 36.80 point explosion from Chris Olave, punching their ticket to the championship game. In the final, Sleepy Nick squared off against the Maryland Marijuanas, but the magic ran out. Jalen Hurts and Jaylen Warren failed to show up, and Sleepy Nick fell short, finishing as league runner-up. Despite the loss, the season marked a successful return to prominence, and Sleepy Nick closed 2025 optimistic about building on that momentum in 2026.
