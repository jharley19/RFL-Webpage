Biography

The Wack Attacks entered the RFL in 2021 following the league’s most extensive search for a new manager to date. Owner Zach Zalovick had no prior affiliation with the league, but his close friendship with congressman Michael Boswell Jr. earned him an interview opportunity. After a last-minute cancellation by Adam Fenton, Zach was given the green light to interview on short notice. He famously left a wedding early and postponed time with his little boo thang just for the chance to join the league. The sacrifice paid off, as Zach crushed the interview and made a strong impression on the commissioner and league leadership. The Wack Attacks’ inaugural draft got off to a shaky start when they selected Chris Carson in the third round, only for him to suffer a career-ending injury in Week 4. However, the draft also delivered a massive steal in the sixth round with Cooper Kupp, who emerged as the WR1. Behind Kupp’s dominance, the Wack Attacks put together a respectable first season. Entering Week 15, they were 7–7 and still alive in the playoff race, and coming off a 200.14 point performance, a league record at the time. Unfortunately, they vanished in the regular season finale, losing to the Maryland Marijuanas due to duds from Ja’Marr Chase and Mike Evans. The loss sent them to the Toes Bowl, where they were soundly defeated by Sleepy Nick after poor performances from Tyler Johnson and Darrell Henderson. The Wack Attacks finished 7th in their first season, heading into the offseason with plenty of unanswered questions.

In 2022, the Wack Attacks entered their sophomore season with optimism, but the year quickly unraveled. The team struggled for consistency and by rivalry week, the inaugural Egg Bowl against Gruden’s Plan, they sat at 6–8, in 8th place, and officially eliminated from playoff contention. Despite the disappointment, the Wack Attacks delivered a statement win in the Egg Bowl, powered by big performances from Trevor Lawrence, Derrick Henry, and George Kittle. That momentum carried them into the Toes Bowl, where they faced The Red Army. In a back-and-forth matchup, the Wack Attacks came up short after duds from DeAndre Hopkins and Adam Thielen, finishing the season in 8th place.

After missing the playoffs in back-to-back seasons, the Wack Attacks entered 2023 desperate for a breakthrough. Instead, the season was defined by decline, as first-round pick Cooper Kupp failed to live up to expectations, signaling the end of his fantasy dominance. The blow proved too much to overcome, and the Wack Attacks struggled throughout the year. They entered rivalry week at 7–7, still clinging to playoff hopes, but suffered a heartbreaking 0.44 point loss to Gruden’s Plan after a goose egg from Gabe Davis. Sent to the Toes Bowl for the third straight year, the Wack Attacks finally earned their first postseason victory, demolishing The Dictator behind a historic 51.50 point performance from Amari Cooper. Their run ended in the Toes Bowl final, however, as duds from Derrick Henry and Gabe Davis doomed them in a loss to The Red Army. They finished 6th, once again entering the offseason searching for answers.

With the second overall pick in hand, 2024 marked a turning point for the franchise. The Wack Attacks crushed the draft, selecting ten players who finished among the top at their respective positions, creating the strongest roster in team history. That foundation translated directly to success. By rivalry week, they were 8–6 and sitting in third place, firmly in the playoff hunt. In a must-win Egg Bowl match against Gruden’s Plan, the Wack Attacks survived by 0.54 points, thanks to a massive 42.80 point performance from Davante Adams and T.J. Hockenson’s failure to catch a final pass on Monday Night Football. The win clinched the first playoff berth in franchise history. In the opening round, the Wack Attacks once again faced Gruden’s Plan and completed the sweep, riding strong play from Jayden Daniels to advance to the championship. In the title game against Big Ounce, the matchup went back and forth before Jayden Daniels erupted for 36.78 points, leading the Wack Attacks to their first RFL championship. After years of frustration, the franchise had finally reached the pinnacle of success.

Entering 2025 as defending champions, expectations were sky-high. The Wack Attacks started strong, winning four of their first six games, but momentum faded late. By rivalry week, they had slipped to 6–8 and 7th place, eliminated from playoff contention but safely clear of the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. In the fourth edition of the Egg Bowl, they delivered a dominant win over Gruden’s Plan, highlighted by a 41.40 point performance from Amon-Ra St. Brown. However, their postseason struggles returned in the Toes Bowl, where they were blown out by The Red Army in the first round after disastrous performances from Travis Kelce and Breece Hall. The Wack Attacks finished 7th in their fifth season, closing the year with lingering questions despite their recent championship success.
