Biography

Gruden’s Plan entered the league in 2017 with a chip on their shoulder and something to prove. However, they quickly realized that they may have been in over their heads, finishing the season in 6th place. Much of their early infamy came from the league’s most notorious trade — a deal so lopsided it became legend. Gruden’s Plan sent Tom Brady and LeSean McCoy to The Dictator in exchange for Marcus Mariota and Ty Montgomery. The trade was so atrocious that it was even mocked on national television by NFL Fantasy Analyst Michael Fabiano.

After realizing that this league was tougher than they first thought, Gruden’s Plan decided to regroup for 2018. That offseason, they brought in former North Point High School kicker Evan Patterson as co-manager, making him the first co-manager in league history. The team also adopted the name “Gruden’s Plan” — a nod to the owner’s favorite NFL coach, Jon Gruden. The new leadership, new name, and a “masterclass” draft that the owner openly roster-baited to only inflated expectations. Reality, however, hit hard. Gruden’s Plan stumbled through the season, managing just two wins and finishing dead last. Their poor performance drew league-wide ridicule, with The Red Army famously declaring, “Gruden has no plan.” Following the disastrous season, Gruden and Patterson “mutually agreed to part ways,” leaving Gruden’s Plan once again searching for a new partner heading into 2019.

A few weeks before the 2019 draft, Gruden’s Plan announced that unstable friend Jacob Kernster would be joining as co-manager. But on draft day, Gruden appeared alone — announcing that Kernster had been “let go” for reasons still unknown. Despite the offseason chaos, Gruden’s Plan started strong, jumping out to a 3-1 record and briefly sitting atop the standings. Unfortunately, that early promise collapsed as quickly as it appeared. The team lost its final seven games of the season, spiraling to an 8th-place finish and yet another disappointing campaign.

Knowing he could not find success on his own, Gruden’s Plan once again went searching for help. That same offseason, the Washington Buttkickers and their owner Logan Handwork were gracefully removed from the league due to a lack of participation. Wasting no time, Gruden’s Plan extended an offer to Handwork to become the team’s new co-manager — an offer he graciously accepted. With fresh leadership and renewed motivation, 2020 marked a turning point for Gruden’s Plan. Early in the season, they orchestrated a blockbuster deal with the Maryland Marijuanas that would later be dubbed the 2020 Trade Rape of the Year — only this time, it was in Gruden’s favor. The trade sent shockwaves through the league, as Gruden’s Plan acquired Patrick Mahomes, Alvin Kamara, DeSean Jackson, and a first-round Waiver Wire Draft pick in exchange for Todd Gurley, Adam Thielen, Zach Ertz, and Drew Brees. Their wheeling and dealing didn’t stop there. In another stunning move, they sent Derrick Henry and a recently injured Austin Ekeler to Babylon in exchange for DeAndre Hopkins, Travis Kelce, and Devin Singletary. These shrewd acquisitions transformed Gruden’s Plan into a powerhouse, rewriting the RFL record books along the way — including most points in a single week (201.80) and most points by a single player with Alvin Kamara’s legendary 54.70-point championship week. By the end of the season, the impossible had become reality: Gruden’s Plan were RFL Champions for the first time in franchise history. The critics were silenced. The doubters humbled. For the first time ever, Gruden truly looked like he had a plan. But was it a one-year miracle — or the start of a dynasty? Only time would tell.

Riding high from their first-ever championship, Gruden’s Plan entered 2021 with ambitions of going back-to-back. But without any lopsided trades breaking their way, the road proved much tougher. A slow start left the team scrambling early, and despite showing flashes of their championship form, they never quite regained their rhythm. Late in the season, they rattled off three straight wins, keeping their faint playoff hopes alive heading into the final week. Yet in true Gruden’s Plan fashion, the dream died at the hands of Sleepy Nick, whose victory sealed Gruden’s playoff fate. Gruden’s Plan missed the postseason for the fourth time in five years, stumbling to an 8th-place finish after consecutive losses in the consolation bracket. In the offseason, major changes followed. Gruden’s Plan parted ways with their championship-winning co-manager Logan Handwork and brought in Ben Hatfield, a trusted friend, to serve as the new co-manager — hoping that a fresh voice could once again reignite the Plan.

The 2022 season was another rollercoaster for Gruden’s Plan. A season full of drama and ultimately disappointment. The drama began at the draft itself, where new co-manager Ben Hatfield shocked the league by selecting WR Michael Thomas in the fourth round. A receiver coming off back-to-back injury-plagued seasons, Thomas was a risky pick that raised eyebrows and sparked much ridicule, especially being selected many rounds above ADP. Many believe this selection was the catalyst for the season to come. By week 7, Gruden’s Plan found themselves at a dismal 1-6 and Thomas was once again out for the season after just three games. In the league group chat, Hatfield decided to vent his frustrations about the draft and the team’s struggles, leading to owner Michael Boswell Jr. removing him as co-manager mid-season. Gruden’s Plan struggles would continue and by rivalry week, they were 4-10 sititng in 9th place and destined to be in the dreaded Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. Despite the poor season, Gruden’s Plan desired to defeat their rival Wack Attacks in the first annual Egg Bowl. Thanks to duds by RB Rex Burkhead and a goose by TE Pat Freiermuth, Gruden’s Plan fell short yet again, finishihing regular season at 4-11. In the Panzerkampfwagen Bowl, they would put up the worst performance in the history of the Bowl, scoring just 174.30 points over the week bout. this would award them the last place finish for the second time in franchise history and facing the first ever beer mile punishment.

The 2023 season marked a major turning point for Gruden’s Plan. After a disappointing 2022 campaign that left them as the league’s laughingstock, the team entered the new season determined to redeem themselves. During the offseason, owner Michael Boswell Jr. brought in a new co-manager, Tyler Bowie, a close friend and trusted collaborator. Together, the duo worked tirelessly to prepare for the draft, developing an intriguing strategy reminiscent of former manager Tyler Ulmschneider’s approach. Their bold plan: use their first two draft picks to select TE Travis Kelce and QB Patrick Mahomes, forming the dreaded Chiefs stack that had powered Gruden’s Plan to their 2020 championship. The gamble paid off. By Week 15, Gruden’s Plan sat at 8–6, just one spot outside the playoff picture. In a must-win matchup against their rival, Wack Attacks, clutch performances from rookie RB Jahmyr Gibbs and WR Davante Adams led to a razor-thin 0.44-point victory, securing a playoff berth as the #3 seed. In the first round of the playoffs, Gibbs once again shined, propelling Gruden’s Plan past the Maryland Marijuanas and into their first championship appearance since 2020. Unfortunately, the fairy tale ended there — disappointing performances from Mahomes, Kelce, and Gibbs doomed the team in the final against Mullet Man. Despite falling short, the 2023 season was widely regarded as a success. Gruden’s Plan finished 2nd overall and firmly reestablished themselves as a legitimate contender heading into 2024.

The 2024 season began with a shocking move from Gruden’s Plan, as the team parted ways with co-manager Tyler Bowie, despite the success of their 2023 campaign. Perhaps a lover’s quarrel? Whatever the reason, owner Michael Boswell Jr. wasted no time naming Joe Whitten, another close friend, as the new co-manager. The duo entered the season determined to build upon their previous success and make another deep playoff run. At the draft, Gruden’s Plan stunned the league once again, selecting QB Lamar Jackson with the 8th overall pick, a move that drew widespread criticism. Yet, Jackson quickly silenced the doubters, delivering an MVP-caliber season that propelled the team to a 9–5 record and the #1 seed heading into rivalry week against the Wack Attacks. Despite a monstrous 46.10 point performance from Jackson, a scoreless outing from WR Cooper Kupp proved costly, as Gruden’s Plan narrowly lost by 0.54 points, dropping them to the #2 seed. In the first round of the playoffs, fate dealt them a cruel hand - a rematch against their rivals. Once again, Kupp’s struggles doomed the team, and Gruden’s Plan’s season came to an abrupt end. They went on to lose the consolation game as well, finishing 4th overall.

Determined to bounce back, Gruden’s Plan entered the 2025 season with renewed optimism. Holding the last selection in the first round, the team doubled down on their faith in Lamar Jackson, drafting him once again — to the familiar chorus of ridicule from the rest of the league. Confident that the right supporting cast could unlock another championship run, the team’s hopes were high. Early in the season, Jackson looked unstoppable, posting three straight 33+ point games and leading Gruden’s Plan to a 2–1 start. But disaster struck in Week 4 when Jackson went down with an injury. Without their star quarterback, the team’s fortunes quickly unraveled. Disappointing performances from RB Alvin Kamara and an injury to rookie RB Omarion Hampton only deepened the struggles. By Week 15, Gruden’s Plan had managed just one more win, sitting at 3–11 and facing another trip to the dreaded Panzerkampfwagen Bowl. In the 4th annual Egg Bowl against their longtime rival Wack Attacks, the misery continued — another loss, fueled by duds from TE Taysom Hill and the Patriots Defense, marked their third defeat in the rivalry matchup. The downward spiral was complete as Gruden’s Plan finished the season 3–12, earning another Panzerkampfwagen Bowl appearance. Their woes continued in the final matchup, where they fell to The Dictator, finishing last for a record third time in franchise history — and once again earning the dreaded beer mile punishment.
